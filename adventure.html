01 / The Concept

The journey itself becomes the interface

During a recent overland expedition across the remote high-altitude Zanskar valley (Manali → Shinkula Pass → Padum → Penzila Pass → Drang-Drung Glacier), the sense of scale was defined not just by photos, but by the continuous transition of terrain: pine valley floors shifting into 16,700-foot glacial passes, crossing remote riverbeds, and longboarding high mountain hairpins.

Traditional travel logs collapse this multidimensional experience into flat timelines: photos, captions, and static GPS polyline maps. Project Drang-Drung explores what happens when you treat the physical expedition as an interactive, miniature world where users discover memories spatially.

02 / The Problem

Why travel memories lose their pulse in digital feeds

Most consumer travel products face a structural design paradox: the physical journey is emotional, spatial, tactile, and unpredictable—yet the digital relive output is almost always a standardized vertical scrolling feed.

Paradigm Shift
From Passive Feed to Spatial Relive
Conventional Relive Static timestamp → thumbnail grid → flat polyline map. High friction, low spatial immersion.

03 / Principles

5 UX principles for interactive relive products

To make the experience feel both playful and emotionally grounded in authentic expedition data, I designed around five core interaction tenets:

01
Explore instead of scroll

Giving users direct tactile control (steering a vehicle through the route) shifts consumption from passive reading to active exploration.

02
Discover instead of list

Embedding real photographs and field notes as spatial landmarks rewards curiosity and mimics the serendipity of real travel.

03
Telemetry creates context

Live altitude tickers (6,700 ft → 16,700 ft) and fuel dynamics ground the stylized visuals in the physical harshness of high-altitude passes.

04
Climatic visual transitions

Lighting and environmental fog adapt dynamically: green valley pine trees give way to rocky crags, snowy pass markers, and turquoise glacier tongues.

04 / Authentic Media

Ground truth: Authentic trip documentation

All photo discovery points in the 3D world map directly to real photographs captured during expeditions in Ladakh and Zanskar—from highline rigging at 11,000 ft in Suru Valley to downhill longboarding on mountain hairpins.

05 / Takeaways

What this unlocks for consumer relive experiences

This experiment illustrates how consumer outdoor and travel applications can bridge raw telemetry (GPS tracks, elevation profiles, timestamps) with emotional, game-inspired visual storytelling.

By designing an experience that takes less than a minute to explore yet leaves a lasting visual memory, we create shareable digital artifacts that users genuinely want to revisit and share with others.

Design Philosophy

Making digital relive feel as visceral as the physical trail

When product design meets real-world adventure, interface conventions can expand. Gamified micro-interactions, responsive physics, and spatial narrative design turn passive travel consumers into engaged storytellers.
